Year 12 Biology: genetic variation

Meiosis: one cell, four unique gametes

Meiosis halves the chromosome number and shuffles alleles, so every egg or sperm is genetically different. Learn the stages, put them in order, then play with segregation, independent assortment and crossing over to see where that variation comes from.

      Segregation

      Segregation is the separation of the two alleles of a gene into different gametes. It happens because the homologous chromosomes carrying those alleles are pulled apart in anaphase I. So each gamete ends up with just one allele of every gene.

      Independent assortment

      In metaphase I each homologous pair lines up on the equator. Which way round each pair faces is random, and it does not depend on the other pairs. So the maternal and paternal chromosomes get shuffled into lots of different combinations.

      Crossing over

      In prophase I, homologous chromosomes pair up tightly. Non-sister chromatids (one maternal, one paternal) can break at the same point and swap sections at a chiasma. This creates recombinant chromatids with new combinations of alleles on the same chromosome.

      How the three processes compare

      ProcessWhenWhat happensHow it adds variation
      Crossing overProphase INon-sister chromatids of a homologous pair swap sections at chiasmata.Makes recombinant chromatids with new allele combinations, breaking up linked genes.
      Independent assortmentMetaphase I (sorted in anaphase I)Each homologous pair lines up randomly, independently of every other pair.Gives 2n chromosome combinations: over 8 million in humans.
      SegregationAnaphase IHomologous chromosomes, and so the two alleles of each gene, move to opposite poles.Each gamete gets only one allele of each gene, so a heterozygote makes two kinds of gamete in equal numbers.

      Together these make every gamete genetically unique. Random fertilisation then joins two unique gametes, so in humans one couple could make over 70 trillion (223 × 223) chromosome combinations, even without crossing over.
